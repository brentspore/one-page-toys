  - Real-3D raw WebGL ten-pin at true scale (60ft lane, 8.5in ball, 15in pins on 12in centres), drag-back-and-flick input, full 10-frame scoring with strikes/spares/tenth-frame bonus. Keys: `bowling_best`, `bowling_sound`. Ticket rule added; **no direct `OPT_TICKETS.award()` call** — the rule would double-count it (same trap as Skee Ball).
  - **The hook is genuinely simulated:** friction is taken from the CONTACT POINT's velocity (`v + ω×r`), with a 40ft oil pattern (mu 0.045) then a dry back end (mu 0.205).
  - ⚠ **THE BUG TO REMEMBER: a vertical spin axis produces ZERO hook.** The contact point sits at `r = (0,−R,0)`, so a y-axis ω is parallel to it and `ω×r = 0` — the ball spins like a top, nothing slides, friction has nothing to bite, and every ball runs dead straight. The spin needs a component **along the direction of travel** (`ω×r = (ωz·R,0,0)`). This looked completely fine on screen and was only caught by measuring lateral deflection.
  - ⚠ **Sequential impulses cannot recover deep penetration.** Pins took >1000 ground contacts per impact and still sank through the lane — that is why strikes were impossible (corner 7/10 always survived, because pins fell through instead of carrying). Fixed with a **positional floor clamp**. Three rules learned the hard way: the clamp must **not add energy** (reversing v.y stacked on the solver's bounce and launched pins into the sky), the lift must be **capped per step**, and **pit state must be LATCHED** — a pin that fell in the pit and was later nudged back inside the boundary got hauled out from half a metre down at 0.035m/step, which is 8 m/s of pure position.
  - ⚠ **Deck friction is what makes a pin TOPPLE** — it pivots about its base. Lowering friction to help pins carry to the corners made them slide instead of falling over. 0.34 satisfies both.
  - ⚠ **Two constants that must agree:** `GUTTER_EDGE` and `AIM_LIMIT`. They were independent expressions (0.467 vs 0.399), so the widest aim sat 7cm inside the channel and **a gutter ball was unrollable**. The aim limit now reaches just PAST the lip — stopping short is not enough, because a dead-straight ball riding the edge never drifts.
  - **Render gotchas, all real:** a plane's triangle winding must yield a **+y normal** or back-face culling deletes the whole lane; **coplanar surfaces z-fight** (lane plane and deck box both topped at y=0 — that was the "flashing wood"); reflections must be drawn BEFORE the lane and the lane blended over them, and **only standing pins reflect** (a toppled pin's mirror sits above the gutter floor and reads as a second pin in the channel); lathe meshes need a **resampled profile + per-vertex normals** or they look like stacked washers; single-sided caps vanish from the wrong angle, so the pin base is wound both ways.
  - **Audio was rewritten after the owner said it was bad.** The pin voice had been 240Hz with 300ms tails — that is a marimba. Maple is bright and dry: a filtered-noise transient plus two inharmonic modes at 700–1900Hz, gone in ~120ms. Roll is brown noise with a speed-tracked resonant peak. **A ten-pin scatter fires dozens of contacts per frame** — voices are now capped at the 3 hardest with rate limiting, which was most of the mush. Still needs owner ears.
