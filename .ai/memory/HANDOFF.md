### ⚠ Audio: modal synthesis is the house technique now (owner feedback, 2026-08-14)

**Owner heard the four new toys and said three sounded "too computery": Claw Machine, Twisty Cube, Glass Harp. Chord Harp passed — and it is the only one that modelled a physical string (Karplus-Strong). That is the whole diagnosis.** The other three summed oscillators, and **a stack of clean sine/triangle partials at tidy ratios is a CHORD, not an object.** Rebuilt in `8833d72`.

- **Contacts are now MODAL:** a short noise burst excited through **parallel resonant bandpasses** tuned to the object's own inharmonic modes. Cube click = plastic at 1 : 2.71 : 4.93 : 7.4, gone in 7ms, plus a low thunk for the cube's mass. Jaw clack = steel at 1 : 2.31 : 4.07 : 6.62 : 9.4, gone in 20ms. Prize knocks are three real materials (plush damped/no ring, plastic short mid knock, glass bright ping). Copy `modalHit()` from either toy as the starting point for any new percussive voice.
- ⚠ **THE TRAP THAT MADE THE FIRST REBUILD INAUDIBLE — peak amplitude 0.00001, ~55dB down — while the mode frequencies were PERFECT** (measured 773/2051/3832 against an intended 1 : 2.71 : 4.93). Two causes: `amp` was applied twice (on the excitation AND per mode), and **a narrow bandpass rejects almost all the energy of a 2ms noise burst.** The excitation envelope must carry SHAPE only, and each mode needs a **`sqrt(Q)` makeup term** (output RMS from noise scales as 1/sqrt(Q)). Click then peaked at 0.29. **Deriving Q from the decay is right for colour and wrong for level — always measure the level.**
- ⚠ **Bells CANNOT be done modally and that is not a compromise.** A noise burst cannot sustain a resonance ringing for a second — measured five times quieter than a click. **Contacts are modal; long tails are ADDITIVE**, with inharmonic bell ratios (1 : 2 : 3.01 : 4.17 : 5.43) plus a noise strike transient. What made the first bells sound like a synth arpeggio was harmonic ratios and no strike, not oscillators per se.
- **A sawtooth through a bandpass is a synth patch, not a motor.** The claw's gantry is now rumble + gear-mesh harmonics + bearing hiss, **all tracking real speed**, and the winch sags in pitch under load. Its cabinet hum gained upper partials because 60/120Hz alone is inaudible on a phone (the Accretion lesson).
- **Glass Harp needed the OPPOSITE fix — do not "correct" its partials.** A rubbed glass is driven by stick-slip AT the mode frequency, so it genuinely is near-harmonic and near-pure; retuning it would be wrong. What made it synthetic was that **nothing moved**: upper partials now arrive only as the glass is leaned on, **two incommensurate slow LFOs** make loudness and pitch unsteady (one LFO reads as a tremolo effect, two read as a hand), and the friction noise is **ring-modulated by the glass's own frequency** so the grain sits in step with the stick-slip cycle.
- **Headless CAN measure level, spectrum, mode frequencies and decay time** (OfflineAudioContext render, or splice an analyser in front of `destination` by wrapping the AudioContext constructor before the toy loads — no debug hook needed). It can never measure character. Both harnesses are worth rebuilding if audio is touched again.

### Reusable WebGL / method lessons (Bowling paid for these)

- ⚠ **Mirrored aim — check this on ANY +z-facing camera.** `mLookAt`'s right vector is `cross(up, z)`, which for a camera looking along **+z** is **−x**, so world **+x renders on the LEFT**; every aim input read backwards and steering was unlearnable. One `SX = -1` at the input boundary fixes it.
- ⚠ **Flat geometry wound to a −y normal is eaten by back-face culling** — that bug ate the lane plane and later the guide ribbon. **Check winding first when flat geometry vanishes.**
- ⚠ **A `readPixels` "proof" is worthless without `preserveDrawingBuffer`** — it reported 0% for every case, including the control. **Screenshots are how you check what WebGL actually drew.**
- ⚠ **Re-measure after ANY mesh-density change, and measure before optimising.** The fps drop looked like new scenery; the real cost was an earlier pin-smoothing pass (~2k → **13.4k verts**, drawn twice for the reflection). **Smoothness comes from the NORMALS, not the polygon count** — `pinMesh(3, 22)` looks smooth and took headless 23 → 41 fps.
